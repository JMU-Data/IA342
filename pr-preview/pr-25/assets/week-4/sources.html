Scientific and accessibility references

NASA Science. “Visible Light.” Original educational resource. Basis for the approximate visible-spectrum range and the light/surface/perception demonstration. Local spectrum colors are display approximations, not calibrated wavelength measurements.

National Eye Institute. “Color Blindness.” Original educational resource. Background on color-vision differences. The unidentified apple-photo comparison is no longer used as the lecture’s scientific simulation figure; the named-model comparison is used instead.

National Eye Institute. “Color Blindness” and “Types of Color Vision Deficiency.” Overview · Types. These pages support the red–green / blue–yellow distinction and the names protanopia, deuteranopia, protanomaly, deuteranomaly, tritanopia, and tritanomaly. The lecture’s cvd-cone-model.svg is an original teaching schematic: it illustrates overlapping S/M/L responses and the conceptual difference between dichromacy and anomalous trichromacy; it is not a measured spectral-sensitivity plot.

Gustavo M. Machado, Manuel M. Oliveira and Leandro A. F. Fernandes (2009). “A Physiologically-based Model for Simulation of Color Vision Deficiency.” IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics 15(6): 1291–1298. DOI: 10.1109/TVCG.2009.113. Official Colorspacious implementation tutorial. The local comparison is an approximate, full-severity matrix simulation using linear RGB followed by sRGB encoding. It is not a medical test or an exact representation of every viewer’s experience. This CVD paper is not cited as the source of the HSV lesson.

W3C. WCAG 2.2, Understanding SC 1.4.1: Use of Color; SC 1.4.3: Contrast (Minimum). Use of Color · Contrast (Minimum). Text-contrast checks are one criterion, not a whole-page accessibility certification or a requirement that all choropleth classes differ by the ordinary-text contrast ratio.

Cynthia A. Brewer, Mark Harrower and The Pennsylvania State University. ColorBrewer 2.0. Original palette tool. Sequential, diverging and qualitative palette exploration. “About five or fewer key categories” remains a practical classroom guideline, not a fixed biological limit.
